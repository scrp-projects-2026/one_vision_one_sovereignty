1. PARTIES

1.1. Lwandile Engineering Projects (Pty) Ltd, a private company incorporated under the laws of the Republic of South Africa, Registration No. 2024/451894/07, with its registered address at 5 GLADIOUS STREET, KINROSS SUBURB, MPUMALANGA, 2270 ("Lwandile"), purpose-built for community empowerment, food and energy sovereignty, and inclusive technical execution, duly represented by Sipho Wiseman Khuzwayo, in his capacity as Director / Founder, duly authorized.

1.2. Inclusive Entrepreneurship Academy (Pty) Ltd, a private company incorporated under the laws of the Republic of South Africa, Registration No. 2022/369284/07, with its registered address at 3103 LEMBEDE STREET, MOHLAKENG LOCATION, RANDFONTEIN, GAUTENG, 1759 ("IEA"), specializing in structured governance, accredited entrepreneurship curricula and community skills transfer methodology, duly represented by Dr. Palesa Charlotte Felix-Faure, in her capacity as Director / Founder, duly authorized.

1.3. IEA and Lwandile are each a "Party" and together the "Parties" or the "Consortium".

2. BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE

2.1. Coal-fired power infrastructure across Mpumalanga is transitioning out of service. Communities that depend on that infrastructure face acute economic hardship and require credible, locally anchored alternatives.

2.2. The Parties have formed a joint consortium specifically to pursue, secure, and execute Just Energy Transition (JET) grant funding, development finance, and philanthropic capital to deploy an integrated bio-solar and circular agrifood model combining (a) agrivoltaics; (b) controlled-environment hydroponics; and (c) an organic waste-to-value pipeline producing biogas and compost.

2.3. IEA brings structured governance, accredited entrepreneurship curricula and community skills transfer methodology. Lwandile brings mission-aligned engineering design, community-anchored procurement, construction and technical execution capability.

2.4. The initiative will be executed in accordance with the Scapush Precision Execution Standard, to secure institutional integrity, SARS and regulatory compliance, and measurable socioeconomic impact.

2.5. The Parties accordingly record their agreement to collaborate as set out in this Agreement.

4. NATURE OF THE JOINT VENTURE

4.1. The Parties establish an unincorporated contractual joint venture (the "Joint Venture") for the purposes of securing grant funding and executing the Project.

4.2. The Joint Venture is not a partnership or agency, save that the Parties' liabilities to third parties may be joint and several where required by Applicable Law. Neither Party may bind the other without prior written authority.

4.3. The Joint Venture commences on the Effective Date and continues until completion of the Project and the end of the Operating Term in Annexure A, unless terminated earlier under clause 21 (the "Term").

4.4. If grant funders or regulators so require, the Parties shall in good faith incorporate an SPV to hold the Project assets and permits. In that event the Parties shall hold shares in equal 50/50 proportions, and a shareholders' agreement consistent with this Agreement shall be concluded.

5. SCOPE OF THE PROJECT

5.1. The Project comprises:

(a) An agrivoltaic installation combining solar generation with livestock & crop production on the Campus;

(b) Controlled-environment hydroponic production facilities;

(c) An organic waste-to-value pipeline producing biogas and compost, including receiving, digestion, gas handling and composting facilities;

(d) Clean water points and zero-diesel bio-solar energy hubs for local community benefit;

(e) 100% step-free, universally accessible pathways across the entire 20,000 m² Campus footprint; and

(f) Delivery of accredited green skills training and entrepreneurship curricula, with a focus on persons with disabilities and vulnerable community members.

5.2. The detailed scope, specifications and milestones are set out in Annexure A.

6. PARTICIPATION, GRANT ACQUISITION AND CAPITAL

6.1. The Parties' participation in the Joint Venture, and in profits, losses and Project assets, shall be: IEA - 50%; Lwandile - 50%.

6.2. Grant-Centric Capital & Funding Acquisition:

(a) The Parties recognize and record that the Project is purpose-built for Just Energy Transition (JET) grant funding, developmental finance, philanthropic capital, and social impact investment, anchored on community empowerment, food and energy sovereignty, and inclusive skills transfer.

(b) Unless the Steering Committee determines otherwise by unanimous consent:

(i) IEA shall lead grant applications, development-finance, philanthropic, corporate social investment (CSI), and skills-development funding workstreams;

(ii) Lwandile shall lead technical deployment, infrastructure asset structuring, and the integration of grant-funded equipment-finance and green-technology partnerships; and

(iii) Neither Party shall be held in breach for failing to secure commercial bank debt, provided both Parties apply continuous, documented efforts to secure available JET, public, and philanthropic grant tranches.

(c) All capital secured via grants or developmental funding shall be strictly ring-fenced for the deployment of the Bio-Solar-Thermal Campus and managed in accordance with funder conditions and the Scapush Precision Execution Standard.

6.3. Initial contributions: Each Party shall make contributions (cash, services, equipment, licences or intellectual property) stated in Annexure D by the dates stated there.

6.4. Further funding: No Party is obliged to contribute funds beyond Annexure D without its written consent. If a Party does not fund an approved capital call within 30 days of written notice, the other Party may (a) fund the shortfall as a loan bearing interest at prime plus 1% per annum, or (b) apply the dilution mechanism in Annexure D.

6.5. Funds management: All Project funds shall be held in a dedicated bank account in the name of the Joint Venture / SPV, requiring two authorized signatories, one from each Party. Funds shall be applied only to the Project Budget and grant conditions.

6.6. Funds received from grant or donor sources shall be ring-fenced and applied strictly according to the funder's conditions and shall not be treated as profit available for distribution.

7. OBLIGATIONS OF THE PARTIES

7.1. Joint obligations: Lwandile and IEA shall, under the Scapush Precision Execution Standard: (a) secure grant funding and technically execute the Project; (b) construct 100% step-free, universally accessible pathways across all 20,000 m² of the Campus footprint, complying with SANS 10400-S and Universal Design principles, with no stairs, steps or other barrier on any route between facilities; (c) provide accredited green skills training modules in Solar, SCADA, Livestock and Biogas, tailored for persons with disabilities; and (d) install clean water points and zero-diesel solar power hubs for the benefit of the local community.

7.2. Allocation of responsibility:

7.3. Lwandile's obligations: Lwandile shall in addition: (a) perform all engineering and construction work with due skill and care by suitably registered professionals (including ECSA, SACPCMP and, where applicable, registered electrical personnel); (b) hold and maintain appropriate insurances including contractor's all-risk, public liability and professional indemnity cover; (c) ensure that all sub-contractors comply with this Agreement; and (d) deliver as-built drawings, commissioning certificates and operation and maintenance manuals.

7.4. IEA's obligations: IEA shall in addition: (a) design and deliver the Training Modules through, or in partnership with, institutions accredited by the relevant SETA, QCTO or other competent authority, and shall maintain such accreditation or partnership throughout the Term; (b) provide structured governance, including policies on safeguarding, disability inclusion and anti-corruption; (c) recruit and support learners and community entrepreneurs; and (d) measure and report socioeconomic impact.

7.5. Each Party shall act in good faith, devote the resources reasonably required, and promptly notify the other of any matter that may materially affect the Project.

8. GOVERNANCE

8.1. Steering Committee: The Parties shall establish a Steering Committee of four members, two appointed by each Party. IEA shall appoint the chairperson, who has no casting vote. Each Party may replace its members by written notice.

8.2. The Steering Committee shall meet at least monthly during construction and quarterly thereafter. A quorum requires at least one member of each Party. Decisions require the affirmative vote of at least one member from each Party.

8.3. Reserved matters: The following require the prior written approval of both Parties: (a) approval or amendment of the Project Budget; (b) borrowing, guarantees or security over Project assets; (c) admission of any new party, funder with equity rights, or sub-licensee of material intellectual property; (d) disposal of Project assets outside the ordinary course; (e) material changes to scope or the Scapush Precision Execution Standard; (f) appointment or removal of the Project Manager, auditor or bankers; (g) settlement of any claim outside approved grant mandates; (h) distributions; and (i) any change to the accessibility, training or community-benefit commitments in clause 7.1.

8.4. Project Manager: The Steering Committee shall appoint a Project Manager, nominated by Lwandile, to manage day-to-day execution within the approved Project Budget and reporting to the Steering Committee.

8.5. Reporting: The Project Manager shall deliver monthly progress, cost and risk reports, and IEA shall deliver quarterly impact and compliance reports, each in the form required by Annexure B.

9. EXECUTION STANDARD AND QUALITY

9.1. All Project work shall be performed in accordance with the Scapush Precision Execution Standard, Applicable Law, good industry practice, and the specifications in Annexure A.

9.2. Each Party may, on reasonable notice, audit the other's Project records, sites and work. Defects shall be remedied within 30 days (or a shorter period required by safety) of written notice.

9.3. Practical completion of the accessible pathways shall be conditional on an independent accessibility audit certifying that the entire 20,000 m² Campus footprint is step-free and universally accessible.

10. SOCIAL IMPACT AND SKILLS TRANSFER

10.1. The Parties commit to the impact targets in Annexure E, which shall include as a minimum: number of learners trained and certified, proportion of learners who are persons with disabilities, proportion of women and youth, local jobs and enterprises created, households served by the water points and power hubs, and diesel displaced.

10.2. Impact shall be measured against a baseline established in the first 90 days, using methods approved by the Steering Committee and, where funders require, independently verified.

10.3. The Parties shall give preference to local labour and local sub-contractors where commercially and technically reasonable and shall design the skills-transfer programme so that community members are able progressively to operate and maintain the facilities.

10.4. Clean water points and power hubs shall be made available to the local community on the terms in Annexure F, which shall be fair, affordable and shall not exclude persons by reason of disability.

11. LAND, PERMITS AND ENVIRONMENTAL MATTERS

11.1. The Parties shall secure, and warrant that they will hold throughout the Term, lawful rights of occupation and use over the Campus, on terms sufficient to carry out the Project. Particulars are in Annexure A.

11.2. The Parties shall obtain and maintain all required approvals, including environmental authorizations, water use authorizations or licenses, municipal zoning and building approvals, generation registration or licensing under the Electricity Regulation Act, grid connection agreements where relevant, and waste management licenses or registrations for the biogas and composting activities.

11.3. The Parties shall comply with all health and safety laws and shall appoint competent persons for the construction phase and operation of the facilities, including gas, electrical and high-voltage installations.

11.4. Each Party shall be responsible for environmental contamination caused by its acts or omissions or those of its sub-contractors.

12. TAX, REGULATORY AND FINANCIAL COMPLIANCE

12.1. The Parties shall at all times comply with all tax and regulatory obligations. Each Party shall maintain valid registration and tax compliance status with the South African Revenue Service ("SARS"), and shall provide a valid tax compliance status confirmation on request and before any payment is made under this Agreement.

12.2. The Parties shall agree in writing, with professional tax advice, on the tax treatment of the Joint Venture (including income tax, VAT registration, withholding obligations, Employees' Tax, UIF and SDL, and the tax treatment of grants), and which Party is the registered vendor and tax reporting party for the Project.

12.3. The Parties shall keep full and accurate books of account for the Project, separate from their own, and prepare annual financial statements audited or independently reviewed by a registered auditor appointed by the Steering Committee.

12.4. Each Party shall comply with anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws, including the Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act 12 of 2004 and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, and shall not make any improper payment.

12.5. The Parties shall adhere to B-BBEE obligations, including maintaining valid B-BBEE verification where relevant, and shall cooperate to maximize the Project's contribution to enterprise and supplier development and socio-economic development.

13. PROFITS, LOSSES AND DISTRIBUTIONS

13.1. After payment of operating costs, debt service, taxes, reserves approved by the Steering Committee and any amounts required by funders or grant conditions, net profits shall be distributed in accordance with the percentages in clause 6.1, at the times and in the manner approved as a reserved matter.

13.2. Losses shall be borne in the same proportions, subject to clause 6.4.

13.3. Reinvestment of net surplus: Not less than 50% of net surplus shall be reinvested in community skills development, bursaries or the Project's community-benefit activities as approved by the Steering Committee.

13.4. Lwandile shall be remunerated for engineering and construction services at arm's length rates set out in Annexure D, which shall form part of the Project Budget, and shall not otherwise be entitled to charge fees to the Joint Venture without Steering Committee approval.

14. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

14.1. Each Party retains ownership of its pre-existing Intellectual Property ("Background IP") and grants the other a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to use it solely for the Project during the Term.

14.2. Intellectual Property developed jointly for the Project ("Foreground IP") shall be owned as agreed by the Steering Committee. Training Modules and curricula developed by IEA shall be owned by IEA, and engineering designs and SCADA configurations developed by Lwandile shall be owned by Lwandile, in each case subject to a perpetual, royalty-free licence for the Joint Venture, the Campus and the community to operate, maintain and teach from them.

14.3. Neither Party shall use the other's name, logos or marks without prior written consent.

14.4. Parties shall, where funders so require, make impact data and open-access educational resources available on the terms agreed in Annexure E.

15. CONFIDENTIALITY AND DATA PROTECTION

15.1. Each Party shall keep confidential all non-public information of the other relating to the Project and shall use it only for the Project. This clause does not apply to information that is public, independently developed, or required to be disclosed by law or by a court, regulator or funder (provided prior notice is given where lawful).

15.2. Each Party shall process personal information of learners, community members and employees lawfully under POPIA, shall conclude a data-sharing or operator agreement where required, and shall apply enhanced safeguards to information about disability and health.

15.3. This clause survives termination for 5 (Five) years.

16. WARRANTIES

Each Party warrants to the other that: (a) it is validly incorporated and has full power and authority to conclude and perform this Agreement; (b) this Agreement does not breach its constitutional documents or any law or agreement binding on it; (c) it is not insolvent, in business rescue, or subject to winding-up proceedings; (d) it holds, or will timeously obtain, all licences, registrations and accreditations necessary for its role; (e) it is in good standing with SARS, the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission and the Compensation Fund; and (f) all information given to the other Party and to funders is, to its knowledge, accurate and not misleading.

17. INDEMNITY AND LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

17.1. Each Party indemnifies the other against losses, damages, claims and costs arising from its breach of this Agreement, negligence, wilful misconduct, or breach of Applicable Law.

17.2. Neither Party is liable to the other for indirect or consequential loss or loss of anticipated profit, save where caused by fraud or wilful misconduct.

17.3. The Parties' aggregate liability to each other under this Agreement is limited to the total contributions made by the liable Party, save for liability for fraud, willful misconduct, death or personal injury, breach of confidentiality, or breach of clause 12.

18. INSURANCE

The Parties shall procure and maintain, for the Term, contractor's all-risk, public liability, professional indemnity, employer's liability, cyber, and property (including biogas and electrical installations) cover at levels agreed by the Steering Committee, with the Joint Venture noted as an insured party.

19. FORCE MAJEURE

19.1. A Party is not liable for delay or failure caused by an event beyond its reasonable control, including natural disaster, epidemic, war, civil unrest, grid failure or regulatory prohibition ("Force Majeure"), provided it notifies the other promptly and uses reasonable efforts to mitigate.

19.2. If Force Majeure continues for more than 120 days, either Party may refer the matter to the Steering Committee and, failing agreement, terminate under clause 21.

20. ASSIGNMENT, CHANGE OF CONTROL AND EXCLUSIVITY

20.1. Neither Party may cede, assign or delegate its rights or obligations without the prior written consent of the other.

20.2. If a Party undergoes a change of control, it shall notify the other within 14 days, and the other may, within 60 days, terminate under clause 21.

20.3. During the Term, neither Party shall, for the Project's geography and business model, participate in a competing initiative with a third party without the consent of the other. This does not restrict either Party's other existing or unrelated activities.

21. DURATION AND TERMINATION

21.1. Termination by notice: A Party may terminate this Agreement by written notice if the other: (a) commits a material breach and fails to remedy it within 30 days of notice; (b) becomes insolvent, is placed in liquidation or business rescue, or ceases business; (c) loses a licence, accreditation or SARS compliance status material to its role and does not restore it within 60 days; or (d) commits fraud, corruption or serious misconduct.

21.2. Termination by agreement at any time in writing.

21.3. Consequences: On termination: (a) accrued rights are unaffected; (b) the Parties shall cooperate in an orderly wind-down protecting learners, community access to water and power, and funder obligations; (c) Project assets shall be sold or transferred and net proceeds applied first to liabilities and grant repayments, then distributed under clause 13; (d) each Party's Background IP reverts to it; and (e) clauses 14, 15, 17, 22 and 23 survive.

21.4. If funding conditions require, the Parties shall ensure that assets purchased with grant funds remain dedicated to the funded purpose.

22. DISPUTE RESOLUTION

22.1. A Party must give written notice of a dispute. The Parties' senior representatives shall meet within 10 business days to try to resolve it in good faith.

22.2. If unresolved within 30 days, the dispute shall be referred to mediation, and failing resolution within a further 30 days, to final and binding arbitration under the rules of the Arbitration Foundation of Southern Africa (AFSA) before a single arbitrator appointed by agreement or, failing agreement, by AFSA. The seat shall be Johannesburg / Pretoria; the language, English.

22.3. Nothing prevents a Party from seeking urgent interim relief from a competent court, and the Parties consent to the jurisdiction of the High Court of South Africa, Gauteng Division, Pretoria / Mpumalanga Division, Mbombela for that purpose.

24. DETAILED ANNEXURES

The following Annexures form an integral and binding part of this Joint Venture Agreement:

Annexure A — Project Scope, Specifications, Milestones and Campus Description

Physical Footprint: Approximately 20,000 m² at Stand number 279 Grootspruit, eMbalenhle, Mpumalanga (Alternative: Stand 353 Witfontein, Siyabuswa).

Technical Scope: Integration of agrivoltaics (solar PV + agriculture), controlled-environment hydroponic production modules, a circular organic waste-to-value biogas & composting facility, zero-diesel bio-solar community power/water hubs, and 100% step-free universal access pathways complying with SANS 10400-S.

Milestones: Phased gate-driven deployment from site mobilization and environmental authorization to structural civil works, technology commissioning, and community skills handover.

Annexure B — Scapush Precision Execution Standard

Execution Rigor: Enforces ceremonial discipline, zero-tolerance for administrative drift, and transparent project tracking across engineering and academic workstreams.

Reporting Protocols: Mandatory monthly financial, technical progress, and risk audit logs submitted to the Joint Steering Committee, alongside rigorous milestone-linked verification schedules required by international and domestic grant-making bodies.

Annexure C — Training Modules and Accreditation

Green Skills Curricula: Accredited modular training programs encompassing Solar PV installation, SCADA smart-grid management, sustainable livestock husbandry, and biogas plant operation.

Entrepreneurship & Inclusion: Accredited business management and cooperative governance curricula designed by IEA, featuring dedicated pathways and specialized coaching for persons with disabilities, women, and youth from transitioning coal communities.

Annexure D — Contributions, Budget, Remuneration and Dilution Mechanism

Contributions: IEA contributes intellectual property, accredited curricula, governance frameworks, and grant-acquisition network equity (50%). Lwandile contributes engineering designs, technical execution assets, land utilization rights, and construction oversight capabilities (50%).

Dilution Mechanism: Grant-centric capital model where shortfalls on approved capital calls are treated as prime-plus-1% shareholder loans or subjected to a non-punitive dilution schedule to protect baseline ownership integrity.

Annexure E — Impact Targets, Baseline, Measurement and Reporting

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Number of local green-economy trainees certified (with mandatory quotas for persons with disabilities); megawatts of clean energy generated; liters of diesel displaced; volume of organic waste diverted; and households served daily by water points and power hubs.

Annexure F — Community Access Terms for Water Points and Power Hubs

Sovereignty & Equity: Free or subsidized tiered access for indigent households, pensioners, and families of persons with disabilities residing within the host communities, with absolutely no physical or economic barriers to entry.

Annexure G — Funding Plan and Funder Conditions

Grant-First Architecture: Structuring applications specifically for Just Energy Transition (JET) funds, philanthropic climate capital, Green Climate Fund windows, and corporate social investment (CSI) grants with strict dual-signature bank account ring-fencing.
